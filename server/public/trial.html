Engine Trial

A trial of three ideas from the production-scheduler proposal, computed from your real, existing data — not a new system, not connected to anything else. Safe to ignore or delete.
What this is and isn't: the full proposal calls for a Postgres/Supabase backend, a React rebuild, and a real capacity model seeded from weeks of per-operation timing data that doesn't exist yet. This page borrows the three highest-value ideas — Ready state, negative slack, workload by assignee — and computes them from what's actually in your database today (Target Finish, scan counts, on-hand material). Where the real spec needs data this system doesn't track (predecessor tasks, drawing revisions, hours-per-operation), it's noted below rather than faked.

Ready

Open batches, split by whether their material is on hand. "Predecessor tasks" and "drawing revision" aren't tracked in this system, so Ready here means: not complete, and not blocked by a known material shortfall.
Material shortfall reuses the exact same on-hand numbers as the Material Demand tab — a batch is blocked here only if its material is already flagged short there.

Slack

Days between "will finish at current scan pace" and "needs to finish by Target Finish." Negative means already behind at today's rate, whether or not it looks urgent yet.
Pace is each batch's own scanned-parts-per-elapsed-day so far — no historical standard times exist yet to seed a real capacity model, so this is a per-batch estimate, not the finite-capacity solver the real proposal describes. Batches with zero scans yet show no estimate — there's no pace to measure.

Workload by Tasked

Remaining (unscanned) parts across open batches, grouped by who's tasked. A rough count, not an hours-based capacity check — this system doesn't track run-time-per-part, so there's no way to convert this into a real over/under-100%-loaded number yet.
